NAD+ NMN & NR

The coenzyme in every cell that declines with age. NAD+ boosting is the most debated frontier in longevity science.

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme present in every living cell, essential for energy metabolism, DNA repair, sirtuin activation, and hundreds of enzymatic reactions. NAD+ levels decline approximately 50% by age 60, correlating with mitochondrial dysfunction, DNA damage accumulation, and aging hallmarks. Two precursors — NMN and NR — can boost NAD+ levels. David Sinclair's research popularized the field, but the evidence in humans remains promising but incomplete. The FDA's 2022 decision to classify NMN as a drug (not a supplement) has complicated access. This is a rapidly evolving space — stay current.

Why NAD+ Matters for Aging
Energy metabolism, sirtuin activation, PARP DNA repair, and the age-related decline.
NAD+ is required for 500+ enzymatic reactions — it's a central hub of cellular metabolism, shuttling electrons in redox reactions (energy production), activating sirtuins (longevity/stress response genes), fueling PARP enzymes (DNA repair), and regulating circadian clock proteins. Without adequate NAD+, all of these processes degrade.
Sirtuins depend on NAD+ — the seven sirtuin enzymes (SIRT1-7) regulate inflammation, metabolism, stress resistance, and epigenetic maintenance. They cannot function without NAD+ as a substrate. Declining NAD+ = declining sirtuin activity = accelerated aging. This is the core of Sinclair's longevity thesis.
PARP enzymes consume NAD+ for DNA repair — as we age, accumulated DNA damage increases PARP activity, consuming more NAD+. This creates a vicious cycle: more damage = more NAD+ consumed for repair = less NAD+ available for other functions = more cellular dysfunction.
The decline is measurable — skin NAD+ drops ~50% between ages 40 and 60. Multiple tissues show similar declines. Camacho-Pereira et al. (2016) showed that CD38 (an NAD+-consuming enzyme) increases with age and chronic inflammation, actively degrading the NAD+ pool.

02
NMN vs NR: Precursors Compared
Two paths to boosting NAD+. Different bioavailability, different regulatory status.
NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide) — directly converts to NAD+ in one enzymatic step. Sinclair's preferred precursor. A 2024 human study (Yi et al.) showed 12 weeks of 600mg NMN increased blood NAD+ levels by ~40%. However: in late 2022, the FDA classified NMN as a new drug under investigation, complicating its sale as a supplement in the US. Availability varies by region.
NR (Nicotinamide Riboside) — converts to NMN, then to NAD+ (two steps). Available as the branded supplement Tru Niagen (ChromaDex). Multiple human studies confirm it raises blood NAD+ by 40-90% at 300-1000mg/day. Still legally sold as a supplement. Better regulatory standing than NMN currently.
Head-to-head data is limited — no large, direct NMN vs NR comparison trial exists. Both raise NAD+ levels in humans. The question of which produces better clinical outcomes (not just biomarker changes) remains unanswered. Neither has demonstrated lifespan extension in humans yet.
Natural NAD+ support — exercise (especially high-intensity), caloric restriction, fasting, and circadian alignment all naturally support NAD+ levels. The supplement debate shouldn't overshadow these foundational interventions. Niacin (vitamin B3) also raises NAD+ at high doses but causes flushing.

🔗 Stacking — Synergies

🏋️
+ Exercise
Exercise is the most proven NAD+ booster. High-intensity and resistance training upregulate NAD+ biosynthesis.
⏸️
+ Fasting
Caloric restriction and fasting activate AMPK, which upregulates NAD+ production. Synergistic.
🔵
+ Methylene Blue
MB enhances ETC function; NAD+ fuels the ETC. Complementary mitochondrial support.
🛡️
+ Glutathione
NAD+ and GSH both decline with age. Supporting both addresses two major aging hallmarks.
💊
+ Resveratrol
Sinclair's stack: NMN + resveratrol. Resveratrol activates SIRT1; NMN provides the NAD+ fuel. Evidence is preclinical.

✅ Checklist

Researched NMN vs NR for current availability and regulatory status
If supplementing NR: 300-1000mg/day (Tru Niagen or equivalent)
If supplementing NMN: 500-1000mg/day sublingual or enteric (if available)
Taken in the morning (NAD+ has circadian patterns; morning aligns with biology)
Completed high-intensity exercise this week (natural NAD+ booster)
Fasted for 16+ hours at least once this week (AMPK activation)
Tracking energy, cognitive clarity, and recovery as proxy markers
Scheduled blood work (NAD+ testing available through specialty labs)
Not relying solely on supplements — exercise and fasting first
Staying current on FDA regulatory changes for NMN
